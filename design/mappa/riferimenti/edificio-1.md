# Edificio 1 — Rettorato (MIA0101)

How the building looks, for drawing it. Gathered from photos and descriptions; see
Sources.

## The building

- Built from 1927 (first stone) to Gaetano Moretti and Augusto Brusconi's design, the
  centre of the symmetrical front on Piazza Leonardo da Vinci. That front runs over
  200 m in three blocks: the Rettorato in the middle, a symmetrical block each side.
- Houses the historic Aula Magna (first floor) and the rector's offices.
- Eclectic, neo-Baroque/neo-Renaissance; a palazzo, not a modern block.

## Correction from the frontal photos

The front is one flat block of even height, not taller corner towers: nine bays, the end
bays stepping forward a little. Two storeys behind a giant order of pilasters with
capitals: a rusticated ground floor (barred square windows, three arched doorways with
iron gates in the middle, a statue in an arched niche in each end bay) and the piano
nobile (tall arched windows over balustraded balconies). Above: a deep frieze lettered
"1863 POLITECNICO DI MILANO", a bracketed cornice, the balustrade with stone balls.
Segmental pediments with a coat of arms crown the end bays; two tall obelisks stand over
each end bay. Low steps run along almost the whole front. The stone is grey-beige, not
cream, on a grey granite plinth. The courtyard wings behind are pale render, lower, with
red-tiled hipped roofs.

## Massing (first reading, superseded where the section above differs)

- Main block: three storeys above a high base, a long front to the piazza (west),
  broken by projecting end bays, each like a small tower, one storey higher.
- Roof hidden behind a stone balustrade; on top of the end bays and of the central bay,
  tall stone obelisk pinnacles (four to six of them, the building's skyline).
- A clock turret on the roof (the clock strikes the hours with two bells brought from
  the old seat in Piazza Cavour, one dated 1863).
- Wings run back east from the ends: lower, plainer, round a courtyard; their roofs in
  red tiles (seen in the 1963 postcard).

## Façade, bottom up

1. **Base** — a high plinth in grey stone, with a broad flight of steps across the
   central part of the front.
2. **Ground floor** — rusticated (fake ashlar, banded joints), arched openings with
   moulded frames; the main door in the centre under a balcony.
3. **First floor (piano nobile)** — the tallest: large round-arched windows in moulded
   surrounds, each with a small balustraded balcony; pilasters between the bays. The
   Aula Magna sits behind it.
4. **Second floor** — smaller windows; a heavy dentilled cornice above.
5. **Attic** — a stone balustrade along the whole roof line, with stone vases/balls on
   the posts; a curved pediment (a broken segmental arch with a crest) over the central
   bay; obelisks on the corners.

## Colours

- Walls: warm cream to ivory stucco and stone (#E8DFC9 – #DCCFB2), darker where weathered.
- Base and steps: grey stone (#A9A79F).
- Cornices, balustrades, pilasters: a shade lighter than the wall, with grey shadow lines.
- Windows: dark (glass reads near black by day), white or cream frames.
- Wings' roofs: terracotta tiles (#B8643E).
- Flag poles with the Italian and EU flags on the central balcony.

## Around it

- The piazza: paved forecourt in front, then the park (lawns, tree-lined paths).
- Iron railings and gates with stone pillars between the Rettorato and the side blocks;
  wisteria on the side blocks.

## For the drawing

- `profilo` bands: base (grey stone, with the steps); ground floor `bugnato` (cream with
  horizontal joints and arched openings); piano nobile tall, arched windows with
  balconies; second floor shorter windows; cornice; balustrade with posts.
- Projecting corner bays one storey taller, each with obelisks; pediment and clock over
  the centre; red-tile hipped roofs on the back wings.
